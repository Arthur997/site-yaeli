Ágape Project

From Adjunct to Opponent

Fluidity, empowerment, cooperation, resilience, practicality, movement, rebellion, empathy, cunning, fidelity, insecurity.

The incredible feeling of opening her eyes for the first time was etched in her mind. The first image of her partner would forever remain in her memory; he waited with a smile on his lips and a welcoming look as she awakened. Together, they wandered through the garden, and the beauty of the place filled their eyes with joy.

He taught her all the lessons he had learned from the Father Himself and emphasized the crucial teaching about the two separate trees in the garden.

– He said that we can eat from all the trees except for the one in the middle of the garden, because on the day we eat from it, we will surely die. He created you to be my partner, to support me at work and be by my side.

In the beginning, Adam treated Eve as his beloved, nurturing their relationship and enjoying the abundant life that the garden offered them. However, as time passed, Eve noticed that Adam became increasingly distant, and their dialogue became infrequent. Her opinions seemed to matter less, and her contributions to their work were undervalued. She was excluded from the meetings with the Creator that took place in the late afternoons, and the topics discussed during those meetings were never shared with her.

Adam no longer kept watch over the garden as he had at the beginning. He was so caught up in his plans and dreams for the future that he lost touch with what was happening around him. One night, while Eve was walking alone, she noticed the presence of a stranger and decided to approach him to talk. The stranger was tired and thirsty; he asked for some water and shared a bit about his life and the experiences he had gathered during his journey. As their conversation grew more intimate, Eve felt increasingly comfortable sharing her own frustrations.

– From what I understand, said the foreigner, you were created to fill a void of loneliness in your husband, your purpose is to serve, and you will always be in his shadow. Your partner is like a god, which is why only he communicates with the creator while you handle the tasks associated with being submissive. Your life has no value of its own and will continue to be this way forever.

As the night went on, she attempted to tell her partner what had happened. However, he was always unwilling to listen, which left her feeling restless and disheartened. She no longer felt like his beloved companion; instead, she felt like a servant catering to the whims of a king.

On Saturday night, while Adam was resting, Eve saw the foreigner walking through the garden from a distance, this time near the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

– I missed you, the absence of a friend who truly understands me. I’ve reflected on your words for days and now see the truth in each one of them.

– You don’t have to carry this burden forever. See this fruit, it has the power to open your eyes to your true role in life. Why remain on the sidelines of human importance when you can be at the centre? Your freedom is within your reach.

– But the Creator said that if we eat this fruit, we will die.

– That’s not true. He just doesn’t want you to eat it because He knows that when you do, your eyes will be opened, and you will be like the gods, knowing good and evil.

Eve observed that the fruit was good, took it, and ate it. In doing so, she became like the gods, possessing knowledge of good and evil. At that moment, she no longer viewed herself as merely a gift to man but as a being of equal value and significance. However, realizing that she could no longer be Adam’s partner while he remained in ignorance, she brought the fruit to him so that he could also eat it.

When Adam learned that Eve had eaten the fruit and could no longer be his partner, he remembered the loneliness he had felt as the only one of his kind. In that moment, he realized he had to choose between being with his partner or being in the presence of his creator. He chose to eat the fruit, as death seemed more pleasant to him than the pain of loneliness. After they both ate, their eyes were opened, and their minds were filled with a mix of evil and pleasure. They realized they were naked and felt shame. As they heard the footsteps of the creator approaching through the garden, they felt guilt and, imagining the consequences of their actions, were overcome with fear.

From that moment on, a dynamic of dominance was established between them, which would persist for millennia in their struggle for power. It was a contest of strength and cunning, characterized by a blend of pleasure and pain, life and death.

Then God spoke to the other Gods:

“Behold, man is now like one of us, knowing good and evil.”
